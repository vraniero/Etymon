## Description (4000 max)

"Notte", "night" and "nocturnal" are one word, 6,000 years old. "Padre" and "paternalistic" meet in Latin "pater". Etymon shows you the family tree hidden in the words around you.

SEE THE WORDS AROUND YOU
Point the camera at a menu, a sign or a book page, or take a photo, and Etymon reads the words and draws their network right over them: which ones share a root, which ones mean the same thing in another language. In AR, the network floats in the room.

TALK, AND WATCH THE WORDS CONNECT
Turn on Talk and have a conversation. Every word said grows its tree of ancestors, in the colour of whoever said it. Etymon works out who is speaking and which language each of you uses, and connects your words where they meet: Italian and English, German and Polish, in the same conversation.

TYPE ANYTHING
Type or paste words and read the story of how they're related, follow a word's journey across the map from where it was born, or explore the whole tree in 3D.

PLAY WITH WORDS
• Root Chain: each word must share an ancestor with the one before. Play against the app or with up to six friends.
• Impostor: three words share a root, one only looks like them. Find it.
• Cousin Hunt and Scavenger: name, or find around you, words descended from an ancient root.
• Birthplace: where was this word born?

20 LANGUAGES
English, Italian, Spanish, French, German, Russian, Ukrainian, Belarusian, Bulgarian, Macedonian, Serbo-Croatian, Polish, Czech, Slovak, Slovene, Romanian, Hungarian, Estonian, Lithuanian and Latvian, and every ancestor behind them: Latin, Ancient Greek, Proto-Germanic, Proto-Slavic, Proto-Indo-European and hundreds more. Each language downloads when you first need it.

EVERYTHING ON YOUR IPHONE
The dictionary, the camera reading and the speech recognition all run on your iPhone. No account, no ads, no tracking, and it works offline.

## Review notes (for Apple)

1. SCREEN RECORDING

The app has no accounts or login, no user-generated content shared with others, and no paid content or in-app purchases.

2. PURPOSE AND AUDIENCE
Etymon shows how the words around you are related through their history: which words share an ancestor (Italian "notte", English "night" and "nocturnal" all go back to one Proto-Indo-European word) and which mean the same thing in different languages. An etymological dictionary answers one word at a time; Etymon connects the words of a real sign, a typed sentence or a conversation, across 20 languages, and draws the family tree they share. It is for curious readers, language learners, travellers, students and teachers: it makes vocabulary memorable by showing where words come from.

3. HOW TO USE IT (no login, no setup)
On first launch the app sets up its dictionary from its Apple-hosted asset pack (a few seconds). Then, from the home screen:
- Type: tap an example under "Try" (e.g. Family), or type a few words and tap Connect. Story lists the shared roots, Tree draws them, Map follows a word from where it was born; Explore shows the network in 3D.
- Photo: choose any photo with text, for example a sign or a menu. The network is drawn over the words.
- Talk: allow the microphone and speak, in English or Italian for example; each speaker's words grow their ancestors and connect where they meet. Other languages are detected and added.
- AR (devices with a camera): point at text and the shared roots float in the room.
- Games: Root Chain, Impostor, Cousin Hunt, Birthplace, Scavenger; one to six players on one phone.
- Settings > Languages: download or remove each language's words.
No sample files or credentials are needed.

4. EXTERNAL SERVICES
None of our own: there is no server, account, analytics, advertising, payment or AI service. The dictionary runs on the device. The app uses only Apple frameworks and services:
- Background Assets (Apple-hosted asset packs) to download the dictionary and its languages;
- Vision for reading text in photos and the camera, on device;
- Speech (SpeechAnalyzer) and Natural Language for recognising speech and its language, on device;
- ARKit for AR; MapKit for the maps; Core Location, only when the user saves a scene, to note where it was seen (kept on the device).
The dictionary is built from public data: Wiktionary (via Wiktextract), wordfreq word frequencies and Glottolog language locations.

5. REGIONS
The app works the same in every region: the same features and the same dictionary of 20 languages everywhere. The interface is in English.
