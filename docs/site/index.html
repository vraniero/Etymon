Etymon

The family tree of the words around you. Point the camera at a sign, take a photo, type, or just talk: Etymon shows which words share a root and which mean the same, back to Latin, Greek and the oldest reconstructed roots.

Common questions

A word isn't found. Its language may not be on your iPhone yet: Etymon offers to download a language the first time it reads it, and Settings → Languages lists them all. Names, very rare words and misspellings aren't in the dictionary.

Talk writes my words in the wrong language. Open the language button at the top of Talk: turn on Auto, or tick the languages you speak. The first time a language is used, your iPhone may download its speech model.

Talk thinks two people are one (or one is two). Voices are told apart by how they sound; similar voices or a noisy room can confuse it.

Does it work offline? Yes, once a language is downloaded: everything runs on your iPhone.

Where does the data come from? Etymologies, meanings and word forms are from Wiktionary (CC BY-SA 4.0), extracted by Wiktextract; word frequencies from wordfreq; where languages were spoken from Glottolog. Etymon's dictionary is shared under CC BY-SA 4.0.
